## 1. Mission

Transform the **existing one-level PlayCanvas Mass Runner at `/3d/`**
from a mixed Kenney + box primitive technical demo into a **coherent
soft-forms / toy-like mobile runner**.

A valid outcome makes the difference visible in a screenshot *at a glance*:
the character, gates, track and hazards all have soft silhouettes, intentional
materials and lighting, and readable, satisfying progression.

**Primary complaint to resolve:** the user explicitly dislikes the current
pixel/voxel/wireframe/blocky appearance and wants smooth, round forms.

Do not mistake a technically working WebGL renderer for product-quality
visual acceptance again. Do not substitute a new engine for art direction.

## 3. Reference contract and identity

**Genre grammar, not proprietary copying**:
- Tall Man Run: immediately readable growth, high-impact +/× decisions.
- Going Balls: rewarding track depth, momentum, camera framing.
- Bridge Race / Stacky Dash: cheerful color blocks and simple navigation.

Do NOT copy proprietary screenshots, textures, models, characters,
trademarks or source from commercial games. Existing licensed
PlayCanvas MIT + Kenney CC0 models may be reused. Newly introduced
assets need a verified license and pinned original source.

Visual direction: **soft toy world / rounded hypercasual 3D**, NOT
voxel, wireframe, pixel art, sharp low-poly faceting or realism.
Desired mood: modern, bright, friendly, substantial, satisfying.
This is a strong target look, not a claim about market-retention outcomes.

Palette *starting tokens* (adjust after side-by-side screenshots):
- sky `#A7DDFF`; mint `#83E3BA`; coral `#FF7994`;
  cream `#FFF0C9`; lilac `#B9A8FF`;
- reward/accent `#FFE177`; deep text/shadow `#304369`.
- Enough luminance contrast for HUD and gate text on a bright mobile
  screen; gate operation values must never depend only on green/red color.

Materials: pastel tinted, mid-gloss toy plastic, gentle roughness,
soft specular highlights; avoid harsh metallic chrome and excessive glow.
Keep bounded light/shadow complexity suitable for mobile.

**Style invariant:** smooth shading alone is not enough. A cube with
smoothed normals still has a rectangular silhouette. Rounded geometry
or a genuinely rounded CC0 model must be visible in outline.

## 4. Implementation: one field, four hero surfaces

### S1 — Avatar (highest visual leverage)
- Replace the current angular appearance of
  `character-male-a.glb` with a genuinely rounded avatar or a
  capsule/sphere-based articulated toy character that does not look
  like a stacked-box placeholder.
- Prefer *one* high-quality licensed smooth character if source,
  shape, rigging and mobile weight are suitable. Otherwise use a
  **deliberately authored game-local smooth** PlayCanvas primitive model,
  not a new 3D engine or another raw WebGL renderer.
- Make head/body/limbs read as one character; subtly oversize head,
  small feet/hands. Smooth shading and a coherent material family.
- Keep size growth driven by the existing `mass` snapshot; visual
  squash/stretch and short settle impulse are allowed as presentation
  only, do not change collision or size semantics.
- Preserve clear motion: idle, run gait, steer lean, pickup pop, hit
  recoil, level-clear/fail. Small state-based transforms permitted;
  full skeletal animation pipeline only if a suitable licensed
  animation already exists and integration stays small.
- Avoid a literal sphere with no character recognition; silhouette
  must be readable in a 390×844 screenshot.

### S2 — Gates (highest gameplay readability)
- Replace current gate bars and posts built by `block(..., type:'box')`
  with **rounded, thick, floating portal shapes**. Rounded tubes,
  bevelled frames, capsules or licensed 3D gate models are acceptable.
- Each portal's value has large text directly aligned to that portal;
  `+4` or `×2` must be readable at a glance, including at speed.
- Positive/worse branches: readable through text, shape/accent and
  relative effect; not color alone. Do not alter the existing
  `applyMassOperation` and gate choice.
- Reward event: brief scale pulse, colorful particle-like local
  presentation or ring wave with tight CPU/GPU budget. No flood of
  occlusive particles. Text must not cover the next decision.
- Ensure labels remain correctly anchored across 390×844,
  360×800 and 844×390; verify `worldToScreen` vs CSS viewport
  / device-pixel ratio mapping on real screenshots.

### S3 — Track and obstacles
- Replace `wide-3d-track` hard-edged rectangular board and
  `edge-left/right` sharp posts with a **raised candy/toy track**:
  rounded lateral lips, bevelled or curved road edge, softer silhouette.
- Use actual curved geometry or licensed assets; do not pretend
  CSS `border-radius` rounds the 3D mesh.
- Maintain wide readable lane, safe horizon, sufficient separation
  between player and upcoming gate.
- Replace hazard boxes with soft but unmistakably dangerous props
  (rounded rollers/bumpers), differentiating via contrast and
  reaction rather than graphic violence.
- Coin pickups can keep licensed CC0 geometry if visually coherent;
  tune scale, motion and material for reward.

### S4 — Lighting, backdrop and UI
- Replace scattered mixed pack props with a *small consistent set*.
  Rounded islands, soft stylized clouds or sparse organic greenery.
  Avoid adding random trees/flags to fill dead space.
- Adjust camera only to improve readable composition:
  player in lower-middle, current/next gates clearly visible,
  horizon believable, no massive empty sky or fake full-screen strip.
- One dominant soft directional + ambient fill, optional bounded
  shadow and restrained specular; no expensive postprocess chain
  by default.
- HUD: preserve mass/score/progress contract and tap targets,
  increase gameplay-critical type legibility. The experimental
  build may simplify the heavy debug/game-factory branding.
  Minimum 16 CSS px for decision-critical values and actionable text
  on a 390×844 viewport; smaller decorative eyebrow text allowed.
- Maintain safe-area insets, portrait-first responsive composition
  and landscape fallback. No overlays capturing drag input.

## 6. Acceptance criteria — hard and soft

### Hard CI and regression gate (machine)
- 44/44 unit baseline and all existing approved W8/W9 browser suites pass
  at their current counts or a higher count; no weakening tests.
- Only `spikes/w9-playcanvas/**` and presentation-local test/artifacts/
  documentation changed; **zero frozen Mass Runner model/level changes**.
- PlayCanvas TypeScript, Vite build and five CC0 asset loads pass, or an
  explicitly approved replacement asset provenance manifest passes.
- Correct gate logic, mass growth, start, drag, finish and replay;
  identical model behavior on deterministic input paths.
- Mobile snapshots captured in **360×800, 390×844, 844×390**, and
  at least ready, running, near-gate, gate-event, result phases.
- No horizontal scrolling, clipping, black canvas, missing WebGL,
  uncaught page errors or off-origin runtime asset requests.
- Confirm 3D shapes are actually rounded in silhouette; automated
  geometry type/mesh provenance tests may enforce relevant entities,
  but pixel-threshold alone does **not** establish art quality.
- New art should not cause uncontrolled bundle growth: record exact
  W9.2D bytes vs W9.3 bytes; review any growth above **25%** for the
  isolated /3d app and justify or optimize before merge.
  This is a review gate, not a claim that 25% is a platform limit.
- Do NOT change the current manual-only Pages publication trigger;
  original default A stays stable; /3d remains opt-in.

### Visual review gate (AI + owner, not silently auto-passed)
Inspect real captures and rate individually (1–5):
1. Character silhouette softness and personality
2. Rounded track and obstacle cohesion
3. Gate clarity and reward appeal
4. Material/light/color harmony
5. Mobile composition/HUD legibility

**Minimum for W9.3 human GO**:
- Owner rates **overall visual quality >= 4/5** and
  identifies W9.3 as distinctly better than W9.2D in same-device test;
- no category rated <=2/5;
- critical HUD and gate values legible without zoom;
- movement and gesture still feel at least as good as W9.2D;
- no serious Android performance degradation or visual occlusion.

Small sample: owner's GO alone authorizes further tests, not a
claim that the game is market-ready. Next 3–5 fresh players validate.
If owner rejects, ITERATE only top 1–3 visual defects; do not expand
scope to other levels.
